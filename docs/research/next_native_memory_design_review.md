# Next native-memory study: remember the goal, keep navigation feedback

**Prospective design review, 6 November 2026. No new bank, policy evaluation, training or launch is reported here.** The completed feedback-acquisition cohort and its stopping rules remain unchanged. This recommendation is a separate, bounded experiment for owner review.

**Prefer withdrawing the saved-goal message over adding a distance reward.** Keep the original square arena, visual landmarks, analytic spatial maps, six actions and 256-unit LSTM. Let the agent see its current surroundings and current spatial code throughout navigation. Ask its recurrent state to retain only a briefly presented goal. This targets the saved predictive goal cue implicated by the original eight-seed interventions while avoiding the recently measured two-second steering bottleneck.

Reward-trained recurrent navigation has relevant precedents. Heess et al. learned remembered-platform returns in a simplified water maze; their disappearing-target experiment prevented movement during cue presentation specifically to prevent storing the target in the trajectory. Wang et al. describe an A3C/LSTM agent remembering a newly discovered maze goal and returning faster on later visits. These support the task logic, not a guaranteed ring or reliable training under our exact optimizer. [Heess et al., sections 4.3–4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/1512.04455v1); [Wang et al., section 3.2.3](https://www.gatsby.ucl.ac.uk/~ucgtcbl/papers/WanKurTirSoyLeiMunBluKumBot2016a.pdf).

## What has already been tested

**Goal-bearing screening and brief-goal training are not new here.** Three existing results constrain this proposal:

- The [original frozen-LSTM goal-family assay](../../results/original_goal_bearing_manifold/pilot/README.md) used four native histories in each of two seed pairs, synthetic geometrically valid goal messages and shared forced physical replay. After one second without the goal input, about 70% of full-state separation remained. Refitted angle readouts recovered the goal, but the earlier readout did not remain aligned and transverse perturbations lacked selective recovery. Native and ordinary goal messages in the same PGC models gave almost equal persistence. This is existing evidence for a changing goal trace, not a reason to repeat decoding as a new finding.
- The [actual-pickup geometry check](../../results/original_goal_circle_cue_feasibility/README.md) used .65 m circular goals. Ordinary/ahead bearing errors were 19.39°/7.81° on Grid's 64 pickups and 19.90°/11.91° on PGC's 63 pickups. These are coordinate-based cue estimates, not demonstrated map inversion or actor readouts. They support a useful geometric hypothesis while retaining every discovery failure.
- The [earlier 5.0048M-frame goal-flash continuation](../../results/ring_rl_goal_memory/README.md) already kept native movement/feedback and trained four-update goal flashes. Improvement mainly survived no-goal input; erasing h/c after the flash cost only .31 score points in either population. Continuous-goal baseline scores were 139.53/128.12 in the original 90-second episodes. Those results support testing the retained controller, and show why another freely moving flash trial would repeat the search/external-memory ambiguity.

The proposed change is **controlled reward-trained retention before any goal-dependent movement**, followed by a single six-second first-return opportunity. The stationary interval also supplies an actual constant-input task epoch for the existing dynamical methods. Actual boundary pickup, true physical target and subsequent actor-driven navigation replace synthetic-message forced replay; they do not make goal-family analysis itself novel.

## One concrete task

| Part | Proposed setting |
|---|---|
| Physical target | Goal center at radius .75 m around the central start; continuously sampled angle; native .30 m goal radius |
| Goal acquisition | An actual first crossing of the goal boundary, with varied incoming direction shared across populations |
| Encoding | 15 recurrent updates with the saved native goal code, while the task holds the agent stationary |
| Retention | Training delay sampled uniformly from 0 through the fixed block cap 0, 15 or 45; qualification and final banks use 45; **all 512 saved-goal inputs zero** after brief encoding |
| Navigation | 90 native action updates: six seconds with fresh RGB and current spatial code; terminate at the first actual hit or timeout |
| Learning signal | Native hit reward only, with the existing +10-to-1 learning conversion; no angle target, ring loss or distance reward |

The stationary interval is an **explicit new task operation**, not a seventh action or a claim that the original arena has a no-op. Do not call `env.step` during it. Hold pose, heading, last travel direction and all motor velocities fixed; use `pa=-1`, `pr=0`. Render the same clean native image and corresponding current code at every wait update, so the retention input is constant. Image noise is zero throughout this proposed task, as in the prior local collectors; native wall textures and pose-dependent rendering remain. Navigation restores fresh pose feedback, native motor noise and six-action physics. Actor/entropy losses apply only to chosen actions; recurrent credit and critic learning include the wait. Record recurrent updates and physical steps separately. The maximum 150 recurrent updates fits one 160-step learning window.

Construct the pickup outside the reward disk, using native forward physics until its first crossing. A simple prospective implementation starts .302 m from the goal center along the reverse incoming direction and takes one noiseless native forward action from rest; its actual crossing, saved position/direction/velocity and absence of wall contact must be checked. Discard setup reward and network history. Reset the navigator to the common central pose with zero motors and recurrent state, independently of the target. Do not use the older inside-disk setup.

Vary incoming direction relative to target bearing. Purely radial arrivals would give ordinary and predictive markers the same bearing and hide the original angular benefit. A boundary marker can be about .30 m from the center, while a .20 m inward shift can move it toward the center. Verify the actual code locations rather than assume these ideal distances. The ordinary marker already lies in the rewarded disk, so PGC may improve robust targeting without uniquely revealing an otherwise unknowable goal. This preserves the current/goal-code navigation connection to [Banino et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0102-6), whose separate grid-network pretraining was supervised.

## Qualify control once; then one finite learning attempt

First seal a fresh 256-case physical bank and evaluate **only the fixed original seed-1 Grid/PGC pair with their original CNN, LSTM and action readout**. Replace only the value head with the same archived fresh critic in both populations, recording its exact source and tensor hashes. The critic does not enter the action computation, so this replacement leaves baseline policy outputs unchanged; it avoids importing the original 90-second task's value predictions into first-hit training. Supply the saved goal throughout the same wait and six-second navigation. Require both to reach at least 192/256 goals, with all cases retained and all setup/physical checks passing. This qualifies this particular control problem; it is not a generic navigation benchmark. If either fails, stop this proposal before memory training. Do not choose another checkpoint, increase the horizon or add shaping automatically.

Use **two separate sealed 256-case banks**, both with the final 45-update wait: one for continuous-goal qualification, one for memory evaluation. If both models qualify, run the full 11-branch original-model baseline on the memory bank before learning: continuous, brief native, brief ordinary, no cue, brief-cue erasure, ±90° donor states, continuous-cue erasure, ±90° wrong cues and exact self-swap. The first two training blocks receive only the fixed brief-native/no-cue/brief-erasure interim evaluation; the final endpoint receives all 11 branches. Original and final useful-memory screens are both retained, allowing the original models themselves to be nominated for the separately frozen mechanism assay if they qualify. No successful intermediate checkpoint replaces the prescribed final endpoint.

If both qualify, train those exact composed initializations, with all weights trainable and the original action readouts retained. **No mandatory competence-training block precedes this.** Use three fixed blocks of 1,024 batches × 32 trials, with retention caps 0, 15 and 45 updates. A separate private stream samples one integer uniformly from 0 through the current cap, shared by all 32 trials in that batch. The cap-0 block always uses zero delay; its stream-consumption convention must be explicit in the runner protocol. The runner records realized delay histograms without changing them after outcomes. Qualification and final-bank delays remain exactly 45.

The first block learns to act after cue withdrawal; the later blocks require the same memory to remain usable at unpredictable release times through one and three seconds. Since the wait and first FREE input share the same sensory state and `pa=-1`, release time itself is not supplied as a cue. Reward-trained recurrent working memory has used variable delays: Song et al. trained with delays of 2.7–3.3 seconds and evaluated at three seconds. That precedent supports varying timing, not this exact schedule or a guaranteed ring. [Song, Yang & Wang, 2017, parametric working-memory task](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5293493/). A cost is fewer longest-delay examples: the last block expects about 22 of its 1,024 batches at exactly 45 updates. Preserve the fixed endpoint and report actual exposure; do not compensate by extending training after seeing results.

Keep original entropy 8e−5, zero injected recurrent noise, the existing optimizer/loss conventions and full-trial recurrent credit. Use a fresh optimizer whose initialization is recorded. Retain every fixed block; judge the last endpoint. The pair totals 196,608 trials, at most 29,491,200 recurrent updates and 17,694,720 physical action updates before early termination. Reserve at most $20 only after the training owner checks a fixed wall-time/price cap; no automatic extension.

## Separate goal memory from external information

Goal angle must be independent of room texture, initial heading, previous action, pickup duration, random-stream positions and cue timing. The renderer in `code/env.py` does not draw the goal, and `CodeSource.__call__` uses current pose/direction/velocity rather than goal coordinates. Still, explicitly audit the complete observation and reset state: native respawn samples away from the goal, so its goal-dependent pose/RNG history must not survive setup. No setup trajectory, reward, pickup direction or clock enters another input. Withdraw the ordinary as well as predictive saved-goal slots. Until first hit, reward input remains zero.

Before the first chosen action, different goals must produce byte-identical physical states and non-goal inputs. Thus post-cue h/c erasure removes the only retained goal-dependent channel. During navigation, position and previous actions can reflect choices made from remembered information; that is normal use of memory, so perform erasure **before movement**.

Reuse 167/256 intact successes and a decline of at least 39 after post-cue erasure as a descriptive memory screen on the new bank. Also retain no-cue-from-start, continuously supplied-goal, ordinary-goal and wrong-goal controls. Confirm goal specificity by substituting genuine **±90° donor-goal h/c states** before the first free-input update, retaining the recipient's physical goal and start. A 16-angle bank supports exact ±4-row substitutions. Donor and recipient goal disks are then disjoint: their centers are about 1.061 m apart, exceeding the .60 m summed radius. Test whether subsequent navigation follows the donor goal. The ±30° disks overlap, so retain those smaller edits for neural phase tests rather than the primary behavioral goal-choice control. Erasure alone can disrupt a controller generally.

For donor and wrong-goal controls, prespecify distance progress toward both donor and recipient after the first 15 FREE updates. Hold an earlier terminal position rather than excluding the trial. Also report which target disk the saved path enters first; actual recipient reward ends the path, so later donor entry is censored. These are descriptive specificity measures, not additional gates.

The old two-second, opposite-heading 50% certificate does **not** transfer to a six-second goal-search task. Do not report that ceiling or silently reuse its gate. Shared-input search controls and goal-specific state substitutions provide the new behavioral evidence; any universal search bound needs its own proof. All old study criteria remain intact.

## Mechanism only after useful memory

Use the first two prespecified physical contexts, dense 768-angle families and actual task histories. Hold incoming direction **relative to goal angle** fixed within each family, storing that independent nuisance as `pickup_offset`, then repeat the separate context; do not average different pickup histories into an artificial curve. Sample goal angle and relative offset independently, so the marginal incoming angle is also independent of goal angle. During the stationary blank-goal interval the recurrent map has constant native background input. This permits autonomous stability analysis conditional on that input; it is not a zero-input experiment.

Reuse the existing full h/c, fixed future-hidden and fixed actor-score maps and unchanged geometry/perturbation criteria. Preserve phase coverage and amplitude; test targeted radial as well as random transverse recovery against tangential retention. A circular set of starting points in a slowly decaying plane must fail the radial comparison. Inspect discrete clusters, extra slow directions and phase-dependent drift explicitly. Slow attracting rings can contain preferred points, as in the LSTM analyses of [Ságodi et al., S7.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00109v2); their angular supervision and state-noise training are not adopted here.

Within the same frozen PGC network, substitute actual ±30° donor states immediately **before** a saved-goal repair cue. With zero-based input slots, initial encoding is 0–14 and blank retention is 15–59. Edit the incoming h/c at slot 30. The primary repair occupies slots 30–44, matching the 15-update trained encoding duration; a separately fixed one-update pulse occupies only slot 30. Every other retention slot remains blank. Give both durations matched blank and unedited controls, and score persistence at the common pre-FREE state at 60 as well as after equal intervals from each cue's end. FREE still starts with input 60: no extra state age or physical time is added. Cross the same recipient states with correct native versus correct ordinary goal codes; those two messages differ only in the 169 predictive slots. A wrong-goal ±60° snapshot changes the whole message and serves as a directional control. Compare persistent phase correction, radial/random controls and native navigation, subtracting each cue's unperturbed baseline. The ±90° behavioral substitution at first FREE is separate. Acute phase repair and autonomous transverse attraction are separate measurements. Between-population dynamics also contain different learned embeddings/background inputs, so they do not isolate recurrent weights alone.

A fixed-radius target has one task angle, but the LSTM can store Cartesian coordinates, a broad region or a few action categories. The goal tolerance permits such solutions. Report the measured representation if the fixed ring criteria fail; do not tighten targets or add noise afterward. A successful adapted-agent mechanism would justify fresh, prespecified held-out seed confirmation and a causal transfer test in original navigation.

## Why not change the reward first?

For this complete-return learner, potential shaping with terminal potential zero telescopes to a state-dependent baseline. Its policy-invariance theory does not guarantee better actor credit here. Plain distance progress instead changes the discounted path/terminal-distance objective; if ever tested, add it after native reward clipping, disclose its scale, and never feed it through `pr` as a hidden goal cue. [Ng, Harada & Russell, 1999](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~russell/papers/icml99-shaping.pdf). The proposed goal-cue task first tests remembered navigation while retaining the original reward and motor feedback.
