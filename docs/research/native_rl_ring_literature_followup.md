# Learned navigation memory: focused literature follow-up

Searched 6 November 2026. This is a prospective methods note. It changes no frozen experiment, threshold, endpoint, or training run. Sources below are original papers or author repositories. Previously reviewed Ságodi, Cueva, and Marraffa studies are not repeated.

The most useful new precedent is an actual PPO navigation policy that develops location-preserving attractor dynamics **when its training includes periods that require holding location**. The search did not identify a verified example of an emergent, normally attracting angular-memory ring inside an otherwise ordinary, discrete-action navigation LSTM. Several superficially close papers instead study a driven loop, a line attractor, supervised path integration, or an explicitly supplied ring. Those distinctions determine which methods transfer to this repository.

| Primary study | What was actually trained | Relevant result and concrete lesson |
|---|---|---|
| [Hawes & Nolan, 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35207), *High-rank connectivity scaffolds support precision and generalisation in recurrent neural networks* | Actual PPO navigation policy; vanilla recurrent network, not LSTM. Stop/go actions on a linear track; also nine-action 2D navigation. | The activity loops through track locations, while fixed-point analysis identifies movement-dependent point attractors and an approximate **line attractor during unrewarded stops**. Supplementary Fig. 7 compares training with versus without zero-speed periods: only the former retains location at zero speed. This is the strongest controlled task-design precedent. |
| [Ye et al., ICCV 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/papers/Ye_Auxiliary_Tasks_and_Exploration_Enable_ObjectGoal_Navigation_ICCV_2021_paper.pdf), *Auxiliary Tasks and Exploration Enable ObjectGoal Navigation* | Actual visual-navigation recurrent policies with four or six discrete actions, auxiliary tasks, and exploration reward. | Fixed-point Jacobian analysis relates auxiliary objectives to the number of slow modes. The authors find dynamics too high-dimensional to identify a simple ring. This supplies a task-relevant precedent for studying the **spectrum of memory timescales**, separately from a ring claim. |
| [Singh et al., Nature Machine Intelligence 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-022-00599-w), *Emergent behaviour and neural dynamics in artificial agents tracking odour plumes* | PPO-trained 64-unit vanilla RNN; continuous move/turn actions; egocentric wind and local odour observations. | Heading and odour history emerge in activity. Lost-search behaviour has a quasiperiodic limit cycle; tracking has funnel-like dynamics. The authors report no fixed points in their analysis. This is a useful precedent for **behaviour-dependent, driven angular dynamics**, rather than stationary angular memory. |

For Hawes & Nolan, changed-speed tests distinguish integration from timing; activity clamping changes stopping; recurrent-mode interventions reveal higher-rank error correction. Their analysis retains roughly half of 162 networks exceeding a performance threshold. All network components were plastic; restricted single-component training failed. Our fixed-head LSTM adaptation is therefore a different acquisition problem. See Methods, Fig. 2, and Supplementary Figs. 1, 2, and 7. No released checkpoint was verified here.

For Singh et al., the sensory stream and the agent's own movement continue to change around the loop. A closed trajectory can consequently be a search oscillator rather than a continuum of stored directions. Their exemplar is chosen from the top five of fourteen trained agents. Figures 4–6 are useful for relating phase, behavioural regime, and Jacobian timescales; the activity plots alone are not a selective transverse-recovery test.

The closest additional RL working-memory example is [Wang & Wang, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11564475/), which trains a GRU with actor–critic reinforcement learning on eight delayed spatial choices. Directional tuning and a stable coding subspace emerge. Eight categorical alternatives and stable decoding leave open whether the dynamical memory is continuous or consists of discrete states. This motivates testing behaviour across continuous phase and novel headings rather than using eight-way task success as the geometry criterion.

## Cue correction: useful precedents, with their actual scope

[Basnak et al., Nature Neuroscience **2025**](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-024-01823-z), *Multimodal cue integration and learning in a neural representation of head direction*, combines fly recordings with a ring-attractor model having plastic sensory connections. Stable visual and wind cues sharpen the bump and improve heading accuracy; conflict manipulations reveal cue weighting and remapping toward a familiar cue. This is particularly relevant to a **correct, absent, or rotated predictive cue after the same state disturbance**. Measure phase error and recovery separately from bump size. It is a biological sensory-anchoring study, not learned grid-to-heading feedback in an RL LSTM. The DOI contains “024”, but publication was 23 July 2025.

[Vafidis et al., eLife 2022](https://elifesciences.org/articles/69841), *Learning accurate path integration in ring attractor models of the head direction system*, learns recurrent and rotation-related weights using a local rule guided by visual heading cues. The resulting circuit integrates angular velocity in darkness and reproduces stimulation-induced heading changes. It supplies a concrete acquisition principle: reliable sensory updates teach what should persist and how self-motion should move it. The model has dedicated head-direction/rotation populations and visual teaching structure; it is a separate path integrator rather than a navigation policy.

[Hasselmo & Brandon, 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3361022/), *A Model Combining Oscillations and Attractor Dynamics for Generation of Grid Cell Firing*, does include feedback from grid cells to heading-angle cells. Its feedback gates **spatial phase** in conjunctive populations and helps create/translate grid patterns. That is not evidence that a grid code repairs an independently drifting head-direction ring. No direct primary precedent for the specific predictive-grid-to-native-LSTM correction claim was verified here.

## What would transfer cleanly to this repository

These are inferences and possible future experiments, not changes to the running protocol:

1. **Train the condition in which memory must be stable.** If the current learners acquire useful memory without attraction, a future study could include native forward/backward delay cycles and require useful choices after them. Held-out delays then test persistence in the trained zero-angular-drive regime. The current common-LEFT condition instead demands memory under continued angular drive. Neither requires an angle-regression loss or a supplied ring. This is a conditional proposal, not a modification of current acquisition.
2. **Make angular precision useful over a continuum.** Random continuous headings, varied cue-to-choice delays, and changed turn speeds can distinguish a continuous update from a few categories or a clock. Keep current inputs and actual previous actions matched at the choice boundary, as in the existing information preflight.
3. **Test writing, holding, and correcting separately.** First establish cue-dependent useful memory; then perturb it during a common-input interval; then apply the correct, ordinary, or rotated predictive cue. Selective transverse recovery, preserved phase, cue-dependent phase repair, and improved native choices form different measurements of the same proposed mechanism.
4. **Allow error correction outside the leading PCs.** Preserve full-state and fixed-output recovery tests. A small-variance component can affect a large-variance spatial representation. If behavioural memory improves without a ring, inspect mode-specific corrections and gated updates rather than converting a closed PCA plot into the mechanism.

Two tempting alternatives should remain explicitly different projects: [Xu & Barak, ICLR 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2002.02496v1) pretrains position/wall memory and then learns an RL readout with the recurrent dynamics fixed; [Saura et al., NeurIPS 2025](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/f12b4df26344f3be803c06b555252efe-Abstract-Conference.html) supplies a ring-based action-selection structure. Both can improve navigation or RL, but neither answers whether the original predictive-grid navigation LSTM learns and stabilizes its own angular ring.
