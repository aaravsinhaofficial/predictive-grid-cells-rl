# Native-policy distillation pilot — proposed settings

This is a new learned continuation of the original navigation agents. The frozen-model results remain unchanged. The immediate question is whether dense feedback from the original full-vision policy can teach its recurrent network to preserve useful information during brief visual blackouts.

The teacher is the exact original seed-1 checkpoint for each population. The student starts from the same weights. Only the four existing LSTM tensors are trainable: input and recurrent matrices, plus their two biases. The CNN, actor, critic and population-code construction stay fixed. The native square maps, six actions, goal pickup, goal-code cache, previous-action/reward inputs, image noise and motor noise are retained. No angle label, additional decoder, controller, prescribed ring or geometric loss is supplied.

At every real student/teacher-mixed navigation step, the teacher receives the full native observation and the actual executed action history. The student receives occasional black RGB frames; for PGC students, half of blackout intervals also replace the current predictive code with the ordinary code. The saved goal code remains native. The loss is the mean forward KL from the teacher's six-action distribution to the student's distribution, at temperature 1. Native environment rewards remain inputs and evaluation outcomes; they do not supply this pilot's optimization loss.

Proposed fixed local budget per model: 16 environments, 90-step BPTT, three blocks of 60 updates. After the first actual pickup, 15 visible updates alternate with independently sampled blackout durations of 2–4, 8–15 and 15–30 updates in the three blocks. The probability of executing the teacher's sampled policy at a decision is respectively 0.5, 0.25 and 0. Both models always receive the action and reward that actually occurred. The three blocks total 259,200 policy decisions, or 1,036,800 native physics frames. Fresh Adam uses learning rate 1e-4 and gradient-norm clipping at 1. The acquisition pilot adds no internal state noise; native observation and motor noise remain. All three endpoints are retained. A 30-minute process limit preserves incomplete evidence without automatic extension.

Before and after the fixed training budget, evaluate 16 held-out native episodes in six conditions: full vision; blackout; blackout plus ordinary current code; blackout with recurrent state erased before every black input; that erasure plus ordinary current code; and the same scheduled erasure while vision is available. Evaluation uses 15 visible updates and 15–30 blind updates, with native goal messages throughout. Also measure KL on all 32 previously archived seed-1 native census trajectories, using fixed masks and the recorded teacher distributions. Those trajectories are independent of the new training stream and supply a paired fixed-input test, not new training data.

Verification stays small: exact native-forward and episode-reset identity; zero full-input KL at initialization; nonzero finite recurrent gradients and actual parameter updates; unchanged frozen tensor bytes; masked-input and Grid-sham identity; one stored-unroll replay check; and retained fixed baseline/final behavior. Both fixed final models receive the existing direction-memory geometry screen irrespective of behavioral success. Original initialization versus fixed final is the comparison; stage checkpoints are retained without endpoint selection. Behavioral erasure and fixed-input replay determine whether a neural candidate is useful. The geometry screen runs separately after acquisition and does not extend training. Live behavior branches share the scheduling rule and duration draws, but their own pickups can renew visibility at different times; exposure counts are reported. The archived replay has exactly time-matched masks across branches. No cloud worker is proposed.

## Primary methods and their scope

[Rusu et al., Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/html/1511.06295) supplies the distribution-matching objective. Their Atari teachers produce Q-values and use a sharpened target; our teacher already produces an action distribution, so temperature 1 preserves that distribution directly.

[Ross, Gordon and Bagnell, DAgger](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a.html) motivates labeling states the learner actually visits. This small pilot uses online KL updates on mixed-policy trajectories. It is DAgger-inspired supervision, not the original full dataset-aggregation algorithm or a claim of its theoretical guarantees.

[Low, Giocomo and Williams](https://elifesciences.org/articles/86943) show that a sequence-length curriculum and gradient clipping can help train navigation networks with circular memory. Their task uses explicit position supervision; this pilot uses only the original actor's action distributions.

[Ságodi et al., Back to the Continuous Attractor](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00109v2) connect robust learned analog memory with slow attracting structure and use state noise in training. That supports a later, separately justified robustness arm. Acquisition is the first unresolved issue here, so the initial pilot keeps internal state noise at zero.
