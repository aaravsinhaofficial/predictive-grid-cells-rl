# Conditional next-study review: learn the route before extending memory

**Design review only; the fixed PGC final is complete and the Grid final is pending.** No new model, rollout, geometry analysis, training or cloud action was executed for this review. Current endpoints, gates and analyses stay fixed.

The unresolved chain is **successful use of retained direction → an attracting circular computation → predictive correction of that computation → improved navigation**. A failure of the current useful-memory gate stops the chain at its first step. It would show that this training recipe did not produce the required behavior within its fixed budget, rather than deciding the geometry of a successfully learned memory.

| Existing evidence | What it supports |
|---|---|
| All 16 original models; pilot methods on seeds 1–2 and frozen held-out tests on 3–8 | Persistent direction information, several slow directions, and little selective radial correction or lasting brief-cue repair in the tested states. The evidence favors changing traces over the proposed ring in these conditions. |
| Blackout continuations, including activity noise | Better dark navigation largely survives every-black-step state erasure. Available inputs, previous actions and physical state can support these gains. |
| Blind-reorientation and matched exploration continuations | Direction traces sometimes affect actions, but pulse repair remains small and full ring criteria fail. Visible rewards, restricted opportunities and strong action preferences contributed to the learning problem. |
| Current local-return study | Reachability and the hard-bank information bound are verified. PGC native hard successes across baseline/stages are 0 → 4 → 10 → 15 → 0/256; all 12 variants score zero at its fixed final. Grid has 17/256 through stage 3; its final is pending. Keep the failed PGC endpoint, not its better intermediate stage. |

The [main evidence record](../../mechanisms/learned-ring-in-navigation.md) and [fixed-stage log](endogenous_ring_run_log.md) retain the results and their scope. The original PGC navigation advantage remains a real phenomenon to explain; these negative ring results favor other computations in the tested original states. A ring learned through new task adaptation would explain that new computation, with a separate argument needed to connect it to the original advantage.

**The head reset and curriculum deserve a controlled test, not an assumed diagnosis.** Resetting the actor removes a learned mapping from recurrent activity to movement. Resetting the critic removes an old value estimate, although that estimate also belongs to a different episode/return structure. All original CNN/LSTM weights were initially retained, but current training updates them immediately alongside the new heads; retention at initialization does not guarantee retention after learning.

The near-start baseline gave Grid 31/256 successes, PGC 32/256 and uniform actions 31/256. That establishes reward availability, not goal-directed competence. Near rewards can reinforce movements needing little direction memory before distance and delay increase together. Yet the preserved original PGC actor already reached 0/128 in the blind local preflight and barely translated. Resetting heads is therefore a plausible contributor, not an identified cause. [Initialization check](../../results/local_goal_return_baseline/report/README.md); [original-policy transfer check](../../results/rl_blind_reorientation_review/local_goal_preflight_v2/README.md).

The current seen controls restore images but still hold the endpoint spatial snapshot during FREE. They do not test normal closed-loop control with both fresh images and fresh position code. The final PGC policy also has entropy near log(6), following training with the larger 0.01 entropy coefficient and sparse FREE reward. These observations point first to acquiring useful goal-directed control, rather than specifically to losing a ring or retaining an overly deterministic actor.

**My finite recommendation, only if the final gate fails: one competence-first curriculum with a matched actor-initialization control.** Keep the same native maps, square arena, saved goal, LSTM, six actions, physics, actual +10 goal reward, terminal rule and full-trial credit window. Keep the final hard distance at 0.58–0.62 m throughout. Use the original seed-1 feature checkpoints, not a selected intermediate continuation.

1. **Learn the local route with normal feedback.** For one fixed 32,768-trial block, provide both native RGB and the freshly updated current spatial code on every FREE input. Use the same forced preparation and hard reachable starts. Freeze CNN/LSTM weights and train only the action/value readouts from actual rewards. Within each population, compare the original actor with the matched reset actor; both receive the same fresh critic, optimizer, entropy setting, trials and tapes. This is four small arms testing feedback-supported control acquisition, with actor initialization as a secondary matched contrast. No angle, target action, demonstration or neural geometry enters learning.
2. **Stop if no arm learns the observable route.** Prospectively require at least 192/256 successes on a separate fixed development navigation bank at that block's endpoint before spending the memory-training budget. This proposed 75% navigation-competence check is distinct from the existing memory gate. If none passes, retain all four endpoints and stop: readout/task acquisition needs resolution before another ring search. No longer run or best-checkpoint substitution follows automatically.
3. **If competence exists, retain all four arms and extend the required history.** Keep their learned readouts, unfreeze the original feature weights, withdraw vision/current-code refresh during the free response, and use the existing single endpoint cue followed by held ordinary input. Use three fixed 32,768-trial blocks with maximum delays 4, 15 and 60, respectively; other task and learning settings remain matched. The complete proposal is capped at 131,072 trials per arm, including the readout block. Every block is retained. During these blind blocks, reward requires the hard goal return; no later visible interval supplies an alternative payoff.

This separates acquiring an action mapping from retaining it through darkness. The actor contrast isolates initialization **within this curriculum**. The proposed feature freeze tests whether existing features support a suitable readout; failure would leave full-network control acquisition unresolved. Short-delay acquisition can still fail. Neither outcome licenses a ring interpretation.

Florensa and colleagues use intermediate-success starts and replay to expand sparse-reward goal reaching: their useful lesson here is verifying acquired competence, not merely counting easy rewards. [CoRL 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v78/florensa17a.html). Heess and colleagues learned memory-dependent physical control and return navigation with LSTM policies trained through time. [2015 primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.04455). Neither validates this particular readout schedule or predicts a ring.

**Do not add a potential-shaping arm as the first remedy.** For shaping reward Fₜ = γΦ(sₜ₊₁) − Φ(sₜ), with Φ zero at success and timeout, this trainer's complete-trial return becomes G′ₜ = Gₜ − Φ(sₜ). An ideal corresponding value baseline shifts by the same amount, leaving actor advantages unchanged. With an imperfect recurrent critic, shaping can change variance and value fitting, but it does not automatically supply new action-specific dense actor credit. This conclusion follows from the actual full-return [implementation](../../scripts/local_goal_return_training.py) and the potential transformation in [Ng, Harada and Russell (1999)](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~russell/papers/icml99-shaping.pdf). Applying nonlinear reward clipping after adding the potential can also break that cancellation and its policy-invariance guarantee. A different return estimator or reward objective would be another intervention; the cleaner first test keeps the native reward and fixes feedback-supported control acquisition.

The new Chen et al. paper predicts position from velocity using a structured place-field readout and reports toroidal dynamics. Its accessible primary abstract supports the importance of the objective/readout, but supplies a supervised path-integration precedent rather than evidence that reward-only navigation must learn a ring. Detailed methods were not accessible in the publisher preview, so no unverified training prescription is imported. [Cognitive Neurodynamics, published September 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11571-026-10555-8).

The final blind endpoint must still meet the **unchanged 167/256 success, 39-success erasure-cost and complete-certificate criteria** before conditional geometry. Report every arm, including a qualifying Grid with a nonqualifying PGC. If memory is learned but the ring criteria fail, useful directional memory through another organization becomes the measured result. If a candidate appears, execute the already specified native-precision, state-swap, correct/ordinary/wrong-cue, transverse-recovery and navigation tests; a candidate alone is not the mechanism.

The owner should decide after both fixed finals and the saved first-action diagnostic. No launch is implied. A successful pilot mechanism would require the settled procedure and a fresh sealed physical bank on the six matched original seed pairs 3–8, retaining failed learners and unchanged final gates. A failed bounded pilot stops this proposed sequence; further changes require a separate design decision.
