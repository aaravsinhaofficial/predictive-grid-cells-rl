Read-only review of the fixed local-return learner, 6 November 2026. No policies, environments, new probes, or final-outcome analyses were run for this review. The findings below concern this new task, not the original 1B-frame agents.

**The leading explanation is a reward-discovery and credit problem, combined with entropy pressure.** The learner receives one clipped reward of 1 only after a successful return. It also explicitly encourages a broad six-action distribution at every free decision, with entropy coefficient 0.01: 125 times the original model's 0.00008. When experienced action returns are similar or nearly zero, the expected action-specific reward gradient is weak; the entropy gradient reduces remaining preferences. Exactly uniform probabilities have entropy `log(6) = 1.79176`, and the entropy gradient itself is zero there. This is a concrete optimization explanation for approaching uniformity, **not a measured claim that entropy gradients dominated these particular updates**. Loss magnitudes alone cannot establish that. [Local losses](../../scripts/local_goal_return_training.py#L374), [fixed settings](../../scripts/local_goal_return_training_run.py#L80), [original settings](../../models/pgc33/pgc33_lstm_s1_1000M.json#L37).

Uniform actions repeatedly oppose forward/backward and left/right translation, with motor inertia filtering the rapidly changing commands. The final goal requires roughly 0.28–0.32 m of travel to enter its disk within 30 decisions; a persistent translation can achieve much more displacement than a uniform action sequence. A committed translating policy can therefore beat uniform even without useful heading information. Lack of heading information alone does **not** imply that all action returns are equal or that uniform is optimal. The hypothesis is that the agent has not acquired enough consistently rewarded action-specific behavior to overcome the flattening pressure. The fixed curriculum also advances distance and delay without a learning-success threshold. [Stages and schedule](../../scripts/local_goal_return_training.py#L40), [native motors](../../code/env.py#L232).

**The task requires a new form of blind steering despite retaining the original network.** During all free movement, the ordinary current code stays at the probe endpoint, the image is black, and reward remains zero until the terminal hit. The network receives its actual preceding action but no updated position or progress feedback. It must infer the goal-relative direction, preserve it through the forced turns, and integrate its movement from action history. This is a substantial change from the original continuously updated navigation inputs. [Input construction and actual preceding actions](../../scripts/local_goal_return_training.py#L255).

The needed information is not simply absent: visible preparation includes fixed wall colors that anchor heading; actual turn actions are supplied; the ordinary spatial snapshot changes after a known four-step forward displacement; and the predictive endpoint pulse depends on that actual travel direction. The native saved goal remains available. These provide routes to estimating the required direction, though they do not guarantee that this optimizer learned those estimates. During FREE, unobserved motor noise additionally limits exact dead reckoning. The endpoint PGC pulse could simplify the heading estimate, but its presence does not guarantee useful behavioral weighting. [Physical preparation](../../scripts/local_goal_return_training.py#L159), [cue and goal inputs](../../scripts/local_goal_return_training.py#L255), [fixed wall colors](../../code/env.py#L353).

The implementation checks found no reward-sign, masking, replay, or gradient-detachment error:

- Actor and entropy losses apply only to FREE decisions. The critic uses all active steps; padding contributes no loss. Forced actions do not directly train the actor or entropy term.
- Returns use the first-hit/timeout terminal mask and zero bootstrap. A hit on the last FREE decision gives the first FREE action a target of `0.99**29 = 0.74717`, regardless of how long the preceding forced delay was. The delay does not add an extra discount to that FREE target.
- Replay reconstructs the CNN inputs and every recurrent update, using the exact stored state noise. State masking multiplies tensors; it does not detach the graph within an active trial. FREE losses can therefore backpropagate through the entire forced delay. This preserves a gradient path; it does not guarantee a strong gradient after many recurrent updates.
- The fixed `100/160` factor multiplies policy, value, and entropy losses equally. It does not selectively amplify entropy relative to reward. The shared critic nevertheless sees 59–99 forced steps before up to 30 FREE steps in stage 4, so longer delays increase its contribution relative to the count of actor decisions; whether that impaired representation learning is another unmeasured optimization possibility.
