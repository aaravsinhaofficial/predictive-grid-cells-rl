# Additional dynamical tests for the original navigation LSTMs

Research note, 2026-11-06. These are proposed tests, not new results about the navigation agents. Existing negative results remain part of the evidence.

## 1. Recover the nonlinear dynamics before looking for a ring

Marraffa, Krause, Mante and Haller (2026) reduce a trained 256-unit memory RNN to a curved two-dimensional spectral submanifold (SSM). Its reduced dynamics contain a normally attracting circle made of two heteroclinic connections between a saddle and a stable point. Activity moves slowly along this circle: a useful ring need not consist entirely of fixed points. They validate the reduced dynamics against full-network trajectories. Their method is developed for neural ODEs; the navigation LSTM requires a discrete-map adaptation. [Primary paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-75924-7), [author repository](https://github.com/alicemarr/RNN-paper).

**Proposed adaptation.** Fit a low-dimensional nonlinear embedding `W(z)` and reduced map `R(z)` satisfying `F(W(z), u) ≈ W(R(z))`, where `F` is the actual full `(h,c)` update. Do not constrain `z` to a circle or train it on angle labels. Choose a small, fixed set of dimensions and polynomial orders using training data; reserve entire histories and perturbations for validation. The first tests are full-state invariance error and multi-step forecasts, compared with an equally sized linear model and a contracting-point model. A curved embedding that only reconstructs training samples is insufficient.

Then inspect the reduced dynamics for a closed slow curve, fixed points, and fast directions leading back to that curve. Validate those predictions by perturbing the full LSTM. For predictive stabilization, start the native and ordinary-grid interventions from identical physical and recurrent states. Measure transverse recovery, angular error, and subsequent navigation under matched future inputs; subsequently repeat in closed-loop navigation. Correcting an angular displacement toward a visible cue is an input-driven correction mechanism. Persistent angular distinctions under identical cue-free future inputs identify the additional memory mechanism.

This adds a nonlinear dynamical model to the previous advected-tangent assays. It does not make shared drift, rotating coordinates, or removal of silent cell-state dimensions new evidence; those possibilities already have controls.

### Discrete-map requirements

The map theorem reproduced in Haller and Ponsioen (2016), Appendix Theorem 5, supplies a direct foundation: `F ∘ W = W ∘ R`. Its assumptions include an invertible linearization, an invariant selected subspace with multipliers strictly inside the unit circle, sufficient regularity, and multiplicative external nonresonance through a specified order. Products of selected multipliers must avoid the excluded spectrum. A continuous-time eigenvalue-sum condition cannot simply be substituted. [Primary paper, pp. 34–35 of the PDF](https://georgehaller.com/reprints/NNM.pdf).

For the native LSTM, evaluate the actual recurrent map in float64 and verify fixed-point residuals in both `h` and `c`. Retain complex pairs together; count all comparably slow modes before choosing two dimensions. Exact unit multipliers, nearly singular directions, or a dense slow spectrum require additional analysis; they are not automatically covered by this stable-subspace theorem. Report the spectral gap, multiplicative resonance margins, conditioning, and the domain where the full-state prediction works. A finite polynomial fit remains an empirical reduction when the theorem's assumptions have not been verified. Natural visitation and basin reachability matter: a remote mathematical equilibrium does not by itself explain navigation.

## 2. Test an oscillatory memory coordinate only if sustained oscillations exist

Pals, Macke and Barak (2024) show that trained low-rank RNNs can store discrete memories as different stable cycles locked to an external reference oscillation. They identify cycles using a Poincaré return map and verify stability through its eigenvalues. This is a distinct mechanism from a continuous ring of memories. [Primary paper](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011852), [author code and trained models](https://github.com/mackelab/phase-limit-cycle-RNNs).

Park, Ságodi and Sokół (2023, preprint) propose persistent angular information in relative phases of oscillators, including a head-direction integration mechanism. Their proposal motivates testing a torus or relative-phase memory if actual dynamics support it. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.12585v1).

**Proposed adaptation.** First look for sustained, reproducible oscillations in full recurrent states under constant native background input. Reject transient ringing and phases extracted from decay or noise. If present, sample successive returns to one fixed phase section. Determine whether the resulting points converge to one state, several discrete states, or a continuous memory family. Call eigenvalues Floquet multipliers only after a closed orbit and a return map have been verified. An arbitrary finite-horizon Jacobian product is not a Floquet analysis.

The decisive perturbations separate common oscillatory phase from memory-relative phase. A common phase shift should preserve the remembered direction; a relative-phase shift should change the decoded direction and actions. Predictive cues should selectively correct the behaviorally relevant error. External periodic replay alone supplies periodic forcing, so any claimed persistent memory must survive identical future forcing across histories. A successful result would be described as oscillatory or relative-phase memory, not relabeled as a stationary ring.

## 3. Locate stabilization in the network–environment feedback loop

Ger and Barak (2025) analyze RNN learning using the joint neural and physical state. In their linear control problem, stability depends on a coupled block matrix containing environment dynamics, neural dynamics, action output, and sensory feedback. Open-loop and closed-loop training therefore follow different trajectories despite identical network architecture. Their analysis motivates separating internal recurrence from feedback through the world. [Primary NeurIPS paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/8acd02d0c55edc3b47a7f9aa42b9cfae-Paper-Conference.pdf), [author repository](https://github.com/yoavger/closed_loop_rnn_learning_dynamics).

**Proposed native experiment.** Clone a real navigation state and apply equal small recurrent-state perturbations. Compare recovery while the agent controls the room with recovery under identical prerecorded observations and with recovery under constant background input. Add physical heading/position perturbations at unchanged recurrent state, and recurrent-state swaps at unchanged physical state. Use paired random action draws when applicable. This separates recurrent correction, sensory reanchoring, and actions that move the agent back into a familiar state. Report the observed mechanism by its location. A circular trajectory generated through body rotation is a coupled sensorimotor cycle; an internal memory ring additionally needs history-dependent phases under a common future input stream. At contacts, discrete action boundaries, and goal resets, compare actual cloned trajectories instead of assuming a differentiable environment Jacobian.

**Implementation check before calibration:** the author's `J` function scales columns of `Wrec`, while differentiation of the stated update requires row scaling. The two continuous Jacobian spectra agree by similarity when the derivative diagonal is invertible, but their right eigenvectors generally differ. Reconstruct the dynamics independently, verify the derivative by finite differences, and validate stored manifold coordinates instead of assuming their correctness. Stored coefficients are degree three: embedding 256×9, reduced field 2×9.
