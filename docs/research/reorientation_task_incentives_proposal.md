# Why the current task can reward avoiding the memory problem

This is a read-only review of the completed 40M experiment and a prospective task proposal. The active 90M continuations and every frozen analysis remain unchanged. No further training or experiment is launched here.

**The task gives a strong reward path around its intended memory demand.** At the final PGC checkpoint, blind evaluation earns 5.766 goals per original episode while earning zero during free blind navigation. Waiting and visible phases supply **98.64% of its goals**. Free blind movement occupies only 10.83% of updates. The agent can largely wait for vision to return and then resume collecting reward. The final Grid agent also earns most reward outside the free window, but collects 0.516 free goals per episode. These are observations of different learned strategies, not proof that the PGC LSTM cannot remember direction.

The [complete reward allocation](../../results/rl_blind_reorientation_review/reward_allocation/README.md) retains all 20 native/seen fixed evaluation endpoints and eight training-stage counter differences. Warmup is excluded from those training differences. PGC training receives only 33, 38, 20 and 15 free-goal rewards across its four stages, compared with Grid's 363, 380, 481 and 624. Those PGC counts are nonzero, so the problem is weak reinforcement of the intended behavior rather than an entirely absent reward path. Global entropy regularization acts on waiting/visible actions as well as free actions; a larger aggregate entropy statistic does not identify which phase changed.

**Physical opportunity is mixed.** The goal radius is 0.3 m; at the commanded maximum speed, a two-second free window permits at most 0.7 m of noiseless travel before acceleration and turning costs. Of 312 final PGC blind windows, 113 begin more than 1 m from the goal center. Their geometry is unfavorable for a hit in two seconds. But 125 begin within 0.7 m and 61 within 0.5 m, and all still fail. The near-window mean movement is only 0.027 m. The seen PGC condition obtains 37 hits in 123 windows beginning within 0.7 m; seen/blind histories can diverge, so these are descriptive opportunity comparisons. Distance dilution contributes to weak learning, while the near failures reveal an additional action-selection problem.

All windows, original episodes, distance strata and source hashes are in the [opportunity audit](../../results/rl_blind_reorientation_review/task_opportunity/README.md). Its nominal speed budget is not a strict bound under native Gaussian motor noise. The separate controlled mechanism assay has exact tape-specific bounds: both Grid contexts and the first PGC context cannot hit within 30 actions, whereas the second PGC context is not ruled out by that bound.

**The credit window is intact.** Every admitted event fits within one 160-update BPTT segment; the worst event uses 129 updates. At discount 0.99, a reward 89 updates after the cue retains about 0.409 weight. Forced actions have no policy/entropy gradient, but gradients from later actor decisions still pass through their recurrent updates. The implementation supplies a valid route for credit. It also supplies many more visible opportunities and a return to vision after a short unsuccessful blind interval. The [actor decomposition](../../results/lstm_reorientation_actor_readout/README.md) identifies the resulting local bottleneck: PGC directional information reaches action scores, while a much larger average right-turn preference dominates the final probabilities.

## One task amendment if the fixed continuations fail

**Train on a standalone, reachable goal-return trial.** Make one reorientation event the whole episode, starting near a known goal and ending at the first actual goal hit or after the 30 free actions. This single change of episode/start distribution has two explicit consequences: the goal can be reached during the tested interval, and there is no later visible navigation that pays for waiting through it.

Keep the square arena, random room appearance and goal location, actual physics, six actions, 512 spatial inputs, saved native goal code, 256-unit LSTM, learned actor and the original +10 reward for an actual goal hit. A known-goal snapshot comes from the same native pickup/code mechanism. Do not add heading labels, a ring loss, a target action, a new controller or a shaped distance reward.

Use the existing labelled blind turns, real four-update forward probe, one native predictive glimpse, common left-turn delay and 30 actor-controlled blind actions. For the visible refresh, use a short forced rotation at the local start so it establishes orientation without letting the policy collect an easier visible goal. All externally imposed actions retain the existing policy/entropy masks, while recurrent gradients pass through the complete sequence. The current 160-update credit window still covers it. The free actor retains all six actions and must physically reach the actual goal.

Sample goal centers and body headings independently, with a continuous range of local start distances rather than one fixed circle. A concrete first design target is roughly 0.45–0.55 m from the goal center at the probe endpoint, leaving 0.15–0.25 m to the goal disk. This scale is a proposal requiring a physical preflight, not a claimed oracle guarantee. Preserve wall clearance, the true probe travel direction, motor state and the actual saved goal code. Retain and report any setup/forced-phase goal hits; they must not become an unreported reward shortcut.

The literature basis is **start-state curriculum**, not a neural geometry target. Florensa and colleagues trained goal-reaching policies from feasible states near the goal, then expanded the start distribution, improving sparse-reward navigation and manipulation without changing the goal reward. Their method uses action-generated nearby states and successful-start selection. The proposal here borrows the near-goal initialization principle; it does not claim to reproduce their automatic curriculum or transfer their results to this LSTM. [Florensa et al., CoRL 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v78/florensa17a.html).

Before any launch, test the proposed trial distribution with the original physics: an analysis-only phase-aware action planner must reach the goal within the free interval across the intended headings, while a common-action policy under byte-identical terminal inputs has a lower success ceiling. Verify common final previous-action/reward/current/goal inputs and controlled motor history. Then freeze every trial distance, budget, reward and control. The decisive learned comparison is successful blind navigation with state intact versus matched state erasure, alongside correct/ordinary/wrong predictive pulses and state perturbations. Merely moving more or collecting occasional blind hits is insufficient to identify memory use.

This would test a mechanism learned in a deliberately memory-relevant variation of the same navigation task. It would not revise the completed original-agent or 40M outcomes. The current 90M experiments finish first; this proposal is not an instruction to adapt their curriculum.

## Completed physical checks

The [first preflight](../../results/rl_blind_reorientation_review/local_goal_preflight/README.md) preserves the initial 0.45–0.55 m proposal. A [separately recorded second preflight](../../results/rl_blind_reorientation_review/local_goal_preflight_v2/README.md) uses 0.58–0.62 m goal distances and central endpoints. This larger minimum gap supports a rigorous paired-history memory requirement under the saved noise tapes: common-information policies are bounded at 50% success, while the physical planner reaches 128/128 goals. Both input populations pass the same identity and geometry checks.

Uniform independent action choices reach 0/128 in the second check. The evaluation geometry is useful for identifying memory, while early training may still require nearer starts to make sparse rewards discoverable. No training parameters or new cloud runs are fixed by these design checks. All current entropy continuations finish unchanged.
